Ask most people what makes a design feel "expensive" and they'll struggle to name it.
They'll point at the type, the colour, the photography. Rarely at the thing actually doing
the work: the emptiness around it.

## Space is a message

White space — the areas of a layout left deliberately blank — is often the first thing
sacrificed when a page fills up. A logo here, a badge there, one more call to action. Each
addition feels harmless. Together they turn a considered composition into noise.

Restraint reads as confidence. When a brand gives an idea room to breathe, it's saying: *this
is worth your attention, and we're not afraid to let it stand alone.*

## Two kinds of empty

- **Macro space** organises the page — the margins, the gaps between sections, the rhythm of
  a grid. It tells the eye where one idea ends and the next begins.
- **Micro space** — leading, letter-spacing, the gap between a label and its value — is what
  makes text feel effortless to read.

Get both right and users won't notice the design at all. They'll just find it easy. That's
the goal: work so clear it disappears.
