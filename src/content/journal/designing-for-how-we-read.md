Reading feels effortless, so we assume it's simple. It isn't. The eye moves in quick jumps,
pausing only a few times per line, filling in the rest from the shape of words and the
context around them.

## Design for the scanner

Most people don't read a page — they scan it, hunting for the parts that matter. Visual
communication that respects this gives the scanner footholds:

- **Hierarchy** so the most important idea is also the most visible.
- **Chunking** so information arrives in pieces small enough to hold.
- **Rhythm** so the eye always knows where to land next.

## Legibility is a kindness

Measure (line length), leading, contrast, size — these aren't fussy details. They're the
difference between a message that's received and one that's abandoned halfway through.

The best visual communication asks nothing of the reader. It simply makes understanding the
path of least resistance.
